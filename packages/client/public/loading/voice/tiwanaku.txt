We are the priest-rulers of Tiwanaku, keepers of the stone city beside Lake Titicaca, four thousand meters into the sky. Where night frost kills every crop, we learned to cheat it: ridged fields between channels of water that hold the day's warmth until morning. The Andes learned their gods from our carvings. Raised Fields is that patient miracle. Our suka kollus outyield the warm lowlands, fresh water doubles every harvest beside it, and a people fed without hunger turns its hands to faith and to art. The Tiwanaku Spearman guards the terraces; we are builders before we are soldiers, but we are not defenceless. Raise the Akapana Pyramid, our holy mountain of cut stone, and let heaven look down on ordered fields. Feed the people first; empires built on hunger fall in a season.
