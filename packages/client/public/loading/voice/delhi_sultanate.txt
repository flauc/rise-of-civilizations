I am Alauddin Khalji, Sultan of Delhi. When the Mongols burned half the world, they broke against my walls again and again; India stood because I stood. I fixed the price of grain in every market of my capital, not from kindness, but so I could pay the greatest standing army of the age. That machine of state is the Sultanate of Hind. Gold flows to the treasury, faith fills the mosques, cheap bread fills the ranks, and my regiments muster faster than any rival can count them. An army that is fed and paid does not break, and mine never did. March the Delhi War Elephant at the front, a moving wall no horse will face and no line will hold, and dig a Hauz in every city, the great reservoir that keeps Delhi drinking through siege and summer alike. Water and elephants: the two things Hindustan respects.
